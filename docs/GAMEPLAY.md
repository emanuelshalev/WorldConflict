# 🎮 How to Play

> *"Lead your nation. Face the consequences."*

You are the newly installed leader of one of **41 nations** in the world of **November 2026**. Every other government is an AI agent with its own history, red lines, leadership style, and an imperfect picture of you. Each turn is **one month**.

---

## 1. Choose how long you have

| Mode | Length | You win if… |
|---|---|---|
| ⚡ **Flashpoint** | 12 months · ~10–15 min | You meet the crisis objective (e.g. *keep Iran below the bomb without a war*) and stay in power. 1–3 ★ |
| 🗳 **One Term** | 48 months · ~45–60 min | **Democracies:** you win the election in the final month. **Autocracies:** the inner circle's review at the end finds legitimacy, stability and results good enough. |
| 👑 **Legacy** | 120 months · multi-day | You survive a decade. History grades your score. |

The **Daily Flashpoint** is the same crisis, nation and world for everyone that day. Build a streak.

**Difficulty:** *Story* (more political capital, gentler coups) · *Normal* · *Hard* (coups are only telegraphed if your intelligence is good). Every nation also shows a tag: **Steady**, **Tough** or **Brutal**.

## 2. Your month, on one screen

```
┌ HUD: leader · month 5/12 · approval · stability · legitimacy · economy · ◆◆◇ capital · tension · score ┐
│                                                                                                        │
│                       THE MAP: your intelligence picture, not the truth                               │
│                                                                                                        │
├ YOUR DESK ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  CRISIS CARD (answer it)        │  ADVISORS SUGGEST (1-click orders)   │   [ EXECUTE MONTH ▸ ]          │
└─────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Answer the crisis.** Most months bring a crisis card with 2–3 options. Arrows show the **direction and rough size** of consequences (Approval ▲▲, Tension ▼…), not exact numbers. Each option shows which minister backs it. Ignore a crisis and it resolves on its own, usually badly.
2. **Give orders.** Pick advisor suggestions, or open **All orders** (diplomacy, military, intelligence, domestic). Tap a country on the map to target it or to open its dossier.
3. **Execute month.** The world moves. The **World Chronicle** leads with *your* decision, then shows what moved and why.

### Political capital ◆

Diplomatic, domestic and covert orders cost **political capital**. You get 2 ◆ a month (3 in a Flashpoint, +1 on Story difficulty) and can bank up to 6. It's meant to be scarce: a reform (2 ◆) is a month's budget, so every month is a choice. Popularity doesn't pay extra capital, and trouble doesn't take it away. You can give at most 6 orders a month, and each order only once per target. Military hardware and nuclear work cost money as a share of your defense budget instead.

| Order | ◆ | Order | ◆ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Court a country | 1 | Reform program | 2 (diminishing returns if repeated within 6 months; raises long-run growth) |
| Denounce | 0 | National address | 1 (less effective if repeated) |
| Offer defense pact | 2 | Spy on / hunt spies | 1 |
| Propose ceasefire | 1 | Sabotage / destabilize / arm rebels | 2 |
| Declare war | 2 | Assassination / back a coup | 3 |

Denouncing is free, but it only rallies voters against an adversary (a historical rival or a country you are at odds with), only the first denunciation each month counts, and it counts less with each month you repeat it within six months. Condemning a partner costs approval, and repeated grandstanding against bystanders starts to cost it too.

## 3. How you lose, and how you see it coming

| Threat | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| **Coup** | A *Coup plot uncovered* crisis. Purge, buy off or concede, before the officers move. |
| **Impeachment / no confidence** | In democracies where the army isn't a political player, there are no coups: an *Impeachment looms* / *A vote of no confidence* card appears instead when legitimacy or stability collapses. Below 10 legitimacy, nothing shields you, not even the promises you made to survive the last plot. |
| **Revolution** | *The streets are boiling* crisis when stability is very low and an insurgency is active. Consolidated democracies only fall to revolution in an armed (guerrilla) uprising with legitimacy under 20. |
| **Defence pacts** | Nobody signs you into a treaty: when a government wants a pact, it lands on your desk as a crisis card. Pacts are rare, never signed with a country at war, and nobody has a formal treaty with Taiwan. If you join an ally's war, you go home when their war ends. |
| **Election defeat** | The HUD counts down to the election; *Campaign endgame* card; the screen pulses red if you trail. Voters weigh approval first, then the economy and any war, and a government that has served three years or more pays a "time for a change" penalty. In the 2026 world, foreign elections fall on their real dates (US November 2028, Pakistan February 2029…). |
| **Objective failure** | The mission chip shows each condition live (○ pending · ✓ met · ✗ failed). |
| **Nuclear holocaust** | Using a nuclear weapon can trigger retaliation chains. Don't. |

A red heartbeat around the screen means you are in danger: approval, stability or legitimacy near collapse, or a plot in motion.

## 4. The world fights back

- **Crises with memory.** Some choices have delayed consequences: deny a scandal and a second leak may follow; reject an ultimatum and you'll find out if it was a bluff.
- **Shocks.** Comfortable leaders get tested: market crashes, pandemics, terror attacks, disasters.
- **Hidden hands.** Rivals run covert operations against you. If your intelligence detects them, you'll get a *Foreign interference* card.
- **Fog of war.** Foreign GDP, stability, military strength and nuclear progress are **estimates** with confidence ranges. Spy to sharpen them.
- **Wars** have a single front line (0–100). They end in victory, exhaustion, or a ceasefire the other side must accept. A side that is ahead rarely stops, but once it is clearly winning (65+ on its side of the front) it will often take a peace **on its terms**: you lose 6 approval and 5 legitimacy, far less than a defeat (−15 stability, −20 legitimacy, −15 approval, reparations).
- **Foreign meddling.** Rival services destabilize you and arm your rebels; the monthly report says when. *Hunt foreign spies* (1 ◆) foils about 60% of the operations aimed at you for three months.
- **Two-strike rule.** A nuclear program still *developing* is crippled by two consecutive successful strikes. Once *tested*, it is beyond reach.

## 5. Scoring

The **grade** follows the verdict: a won session is never below C, a failed or deposed one never above D, and an early failure is an F; within that band, score versus par decides. "Stay in power" is only failed if you were removed.

You play as the era's **real leader** by default (type a name on the title screen to be someone else).

Economy: growth shocks (oil spikes, crises, financial panics) fade back toward each country's structural trend within about a year, and voters judge growth against what is normal for *their* country. The growth figure in the HUD is **real growth**: what actually happened to GDP, including war damage, crisis hits and reparations. A ground war on your own soil is ruinous; a distant air and naval campaign costs far less. Defence budgets rise when rivals mobilise, but only to about one and a half times their peacetime level without a war. Stability, legitimacy and approval are hard to keep at the very top and recover slowly from the very bottom.

Four components, 0–50 each: **Economy**, **Security**, **Approval**, **Prestige** (total 0–200, start 100). They drift each month toward what your situation deserves, so sustained performance matters more than one good month. Your end screen shows a **grade**, **stars**, a leadership **archetype** read from what you actually did (*Warmonger*, *The Gambler*, *Shadow Operator*, *Iron Fist*, *Peacemaker*, *The Reformer*, *Master Diplomat*, *Steady Hand*…), a month-by-month emoji strip, and, for flashpoints, what really happened.

---

## 6. Tips

- **Read the arrows twice.** The option that helps approval today often costs you legitimacy or allies tomorrow.
- **Don't spam national addresses.** Each repeat within six months works 40% less, and the press notices.
- **Popularity breeds trouble.** Very popular leaders attract scandals and hubris.
- **Allies matter.** Defense pacts deter, and drag you into their wars.
- **Talk to your cabinet.** Each minister reasons from their department and from your intelligence, so they will disagree.
