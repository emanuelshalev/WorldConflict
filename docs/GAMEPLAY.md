# 🎮 How to Play

> *"Lead your nation. Face the consequences."*

You are the newly installed leader of one of **41 nations** in the world of **November 2026**. Every other government is an AI agent with its own history, red lines, leadership style, and an imperfect picture of you. Each turn is **one month**.

---

## 1. Choose how long you have

| Mode | Length | You win if… |
|---|---|---|
| ⚡ **Flashpoint** | 12 months · ~10–15 min | You meet the crisis objective (e.g. *keep Iran below the bomb without a war*) and stay in power. 1–3 ★ |
| 🗳 **One Term** | 48 months · ~45–60 min | **Democracies:** you win the election in the final month. **Autocracies:** the inner circle's review at the end finds legitimacy, stability and results good enough. |
| 👑 **Legacy** | 120 months · multi-day | You survive a decade. History grades your score. |

The **Daily Flashpoint** is the same crisis, nation and world for everyone that day. Build a streak.

### Pick an era and a nation

The title screen is a globe. In a Flashpoint it pins every crisis: pick one, then a side. In One Term and Legacy it shows the world of the era you pick (1960, 1990, 2025 or 2026): choosing another era turns the globe and rolls the year to it. The globe shows the era's main situations ("This era's 5 fires", or all of them and the rival lines with one switch), colours countries by bloc, stability or difficulty, and paints borders as they were then (the Soviet Union as one country, East Germany, South Yemen, era names such as Zaire). Click a country for its card: its leader in that era, its bloc, and **Lead** it. A situation on the globe can open as a 15-minute Flashpoint when one covers it.

You govern as the era's real leader by default: Eisenhower, Khrushchev or de Gaulle in 1960, Bush, Gorbachev or Thatcher in 1990, Trump, Putin or Scholz in January 2025, and the leaders in office in November 2026. A few leaders are invented for an era and say so. Nations that were not sovereign states at the time (Ukraine and Belarus inside the Soviet Union in 1960 and 1990, Syria inside the United Arab Republic and Nigeria before independence in 1960, the Trucial States before the UAE, and the West Bank under Jordan in 1960) are greyed out with a "not playable" note: they stay on the map, but have no foreign policy of their own in that era. Tick **What if** under the nation list to lead one of them anyway as alternate history (off by default, labelled "What if" on the nation, the launch button and the save): your nation is a sovereign state in that game, and the rest of the era stays as it was. The West Bank is a normal seat in 1990 (the PLO's State of Palestine), 2025 and 2026.

In **One Term**, the optional **Start with** list picks one of the era's active situations (the war in Ukraine, Hormuz, Taiwan...): your first crisis is about it, framed from your seat (a side in it, or an outsider deciding whether to back one side, broker talks or stay out). Leave it on "Whatever the first month brings" for the usual opening. The same seed and pick always give the same opening.

**Difficulty:** *Story* (more political capital, gentler coups) · *Normal* · *Hard* (coups are only telegraphed if your intelligence is good). Every nation also shows a tag: **Steady**, **Tough** or **Brutal**.

## 2. Your month, on one screen

The game has two views, switched at the top right or with **M**:

- **Desk** (where every month starts): you decide.
- **Map**: the full globe with its layers, dossiers and legend, to investigate the world. The current thing of the month stays on a card on the left with the same answers, and what will happen stays on the right (on smaller screens, a bar along the bottom with **Review 7 recommended** and **Execute**), so you can finish a month from here too. Pressing **Desk** (or **M**) always goes back to the desk and closes an open order menu. Order cards on the map, the desk and the dossier are the same card, with the same buttons and words. **Whole world** zooms back out; country names appear as you zoom in.

The screen uses all of its width and never hides what you must see in a box that scrolls. A big screen gets a bigger game with more side by side, not an emptier one; a small screen folds things into a visible summary with a count ("See all 7", "Review 7 recommended") instead of hiding them.

```
+ STATE: who | Month 4 of 12 | [Mission: ... 3 of 5 goals on track  Open] | political capital ◆ 3 of 6 [Explain] | orders | meetings | defense budget | approval | stability | world tension | [Desk|Map] [Menu] +
+ MONTH BAR: 2 of 7: Xi Jinping calls / 1 done, 6 to go        Show: [One at a time | Everything]  [Back] [Next: ...] [Check and execute] +
| THIS MONTH: 7 THINGS   | THE CURRENT THING                                         | WHAT WILL HAPPEN            |
|  Tanker hit in ... Open|  the scene, how it got here | the answers, each [Choose], | Decided (1)                 |
|  Xi Jinping calls  Open|  your goals in this call    | Skip as the last card       | Recommended (5), still to do|
|  Small meetings    Open|                            | what your advisers say      |  each with [Open]           |
|  Recommended orders    |  (wide screens: the subject map and the background)     | [Choose all recommended (5)]|
|  Your own orders  Open |                                                         | If you execute now: ...     |
|  Your mission          |                                                         | [Execute month 4]           |
+ NEWS TICKER (hide with N) ---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**The state band** at the top never moves and never scrolls: who you are, the month, your mission (one button that opens the **Mission sheet** with each goal and its state), political capital with **Explain**, the orders, meetings and defense budget you have used this month, and your country (approval, stability, world tension, and **Score**, which opens why your score moved). When something you do costs political capital, the number changes in place.

**The month is a short list of things**, always in the same order and with the same words on the desk, on the map and on a phone: the crisis first, then the calls and visits that matter, then the **small meetings** in one group (they need no decision from you), then the **recommended orders** your advisers propose, then **your own orders** (optional). The month bar names where you are ("2 of 7: Xi Jinping calls", "1 done, 6 to go") with **Back**, **Next** (it names the next thing), **See all** (when the list is not already on screen) and **Check and execute**. Each thing ends with your decision:

1. **Choose** an answer. Every answer is one card; press anywhere on it. Each card says the goal it **Helps**, **Hurts** or **Breaks** and its biggest effects; on tall screens it also says what it costs and who backs it, and **How much?** shows rough sizes ("Approval about -3"). The answer your advisers recommend says so in a plain line on the card ("★ Recommended by your Defense Minister: ..."); when it hurts your mission the screen says so. On Story difficulty the cabinet never picks against your mission. **Ask your staff** opens the right minister with the question already asked.
2. Or **Skip**, the last card of every list, which says what happens instead and what it costs: for a crisis your cabinet goes with its recommended answer, for one of your calls it says what leaving it open does (below), for a meeting your minister answers ("Free, but China -3: Xi notices you did not call back"), recommended orders do not run (the political capital stays). A skipped thing says **Skipped** with **Undo**.

In **One at a time** (the default) the next thing opens after each choice; when nothing is left, the **Check what will happen** page lists what you chose, what is left to your advisers and what you skipped, each with **Change** or **Undo**, and **Execute**. **Everything** keeps every thing in view and moves on only when you say so. Phones show one thing per screen, with **Next** at the bottom and a line that counts the answers still below ("2 more answers and Skip below"); the review screen holds the only **Execute** button.

**Your call.** One or two things a month are yours alone: the crisis your advisers disagree on (its answers differ for your mission), and **the move for your mission** (the orders your advisers propose to push a goal, plus **Hold this month**). They are marked **Your call** in the list, the month bar and the state band ("Your calls 2 open"). On a call each card names the advisers who recommend it ("Recommended by your Foreign Minister") and no answer is starred; an answer on the mission side may cost ◆ 1 more, shown on its card; at Guide me the Chief of Staff says which one serves the mission and which keeps things calm. A call starts with no answer: no card looks chosen, and the screen says what leaving it does ("No answer yet. If you leave it: Break the strike", with its cost in political capital when it has one; before the crisis is due, "No answer yet. If you leave it, the crisis passes with no effect"). Its Skip card says the same. Choose all recommended never decides a call: when nothing else is left the greyed-out button names it ("Nothing else to choose. Still open: General strike"), and if you execute with one open, it says so first. Settings, **Let the Chief of Staff make my calls** (off by default): each call starts on the Chief of Staff's answer (the one that serves your mission, or the calm one when political capital is short) and Choose all recommended chooses it too; a crisis call you have not answered follows the setting at once, the order for your mission from the next month.

**Orders.** **Your own orders** lists the orders your advisers think fit (**Orders to consider**, each with one line on why now) and **All orders** (search, or browse by family). When you have ◆ 3 or more unspent, a **Big move** suggests up to three orders that help the mission together ("Do it"). Your advisers also recommend up to two safe orders each month (never a long shot or a repeat of last month, always leaving ◆ 1): each shows what it will change ("Will change: Approval about +4") and spends no political capital until you choose it (its card says "◆ 1 if chosen"); one you have not chosen does not run. Remove any of them, or turn them off in Settings ("Staff propose orders").

**What will happen** (on the right; on tablets the bar along the bottom) always shows what you decided, what your advisers recommend for the things still to do, and what you skipped, then **If you execute now:** in plain words ("Your ministers take 4 meetings for you: Germany -1, Russia -1"; "The 2 recommended orders do not run") above **Execute month 4** (Ctrl+Enter), which ends the month. Each recommendation names its adviser ("Recommended by Foreign Minister") and what it will change, with **Open** to go to that thing and choose there. **Choose all recommended (5)** (Shift+A, while the list is on screen) chooses the recommended answer for every thing still to do; it never touches what you decided or skipped, and when nothing waits it is greyed out and says why. When the list does not fit, sections fold to a heading with their count and a one-line summary, and **Show** opens one; on tablets and in Map mode **Review 5 recommended** opens the list first, so nothing is chosen unseen.

Month 1 is quieter: the list holds only the crisis and the recommended orders, your ministers take that month's meetings (no effect; **See the meetings anyway** adds them), and **Your goal** names in one sentence every goal you are judged on ("You win if all five hold at the end of month 12: the shooting stops, ... and Israel stays on side (relations 61+)"). A three-step guide points at your move, your advisors and Execute the first time (Settings: **Show me around again**).

**After Execute: the result card.** A big month (a war, a nuclear strike, a coup, a fall from power, a peace, a mission complete) first plays a short full-screen moment with its own sound; any key skips it, and there is at most one a month. Then the **result card** ("What your choice did"): what your move changed, your mission row, the follow-ups that will come due ("comes in 2 months; we will tell you"), **Why your score moved**, and a thumbnail of the paper. Every order, crisis answer, meeting and vote ends with one **status**, the same word on every screen (the stamp in the details, the result card, the paper's "your month" list): **Done**, **Partly done** (it went through, but part of it did not: talks stalled, the operation was exposed, an order your answer set off failed), **Failed** (it was tried and did not work: the ceasefire was rejected, the strike was repelled), **Refused** (a rule stopped it before anything happened: no capital, not at war) or **No effect** (it went through and nothing measurable moved). A cost is not a failure: a call-up that raised mobilization and cost 2 approval is *Done, at a cost*. "What your choice did" is always your own move; a front that moved by itself is shown on its own line ("Not from your choice"), and other countries' moves carry no stamp. **Next month** goes straight back to the desk; **See everything** opens the details (the outcomes grouped as the month, diplomacy by country, and home and security, with what each side did and the numbers that moved); **Read the paper** opens the World Chronicle, which leads with *your* decision. Settings, **After each month**, chooses between the result card only (the default), the card and the details, or everything including the paper.

**Sound.** The game makes its own sounds (taps, the stamp when you add an order, the spend of political capital, a refusal, Execute, and a short sting for big moments). It is on by default; mute it from the speaker button at the top or set the volume in Settings.

Your choices are saved as you go, so a reload brings back the same desk. Keys: M (Map/Desk), J/K (next/previous thing), 1-4 (choose an answer), S (skip), Enter (choose the recommended answer), Delete (remove the order in view), / (search orders, Up/Down and Enter), Shift+A (choose all recommended, while the list is on screen), G (map legend), N (news ticker), I (hide or show name underlines), Ctrl+Enter (execute).

### Your calendar

Every month also brings 3 to 5 meetings on the desk's calendar:

- **Calls** from other leaders, chosen from who matters to you right now: an enemy on a secure line, a rival with a warning, an ally asking for support, a partner offering a deal. Nobody calls two months running.
- **A staff meeting:** the most pressing problem your ministers can see (the front, the polls, the economy, unrest, a spy, a back channel, a leak).
- **A state visit** or **a prime-time interview**, alternating.

Each meeting is a short scene with a statement and 2 or 3 answers with their effects shown. You take up to **3 meetings** a month yourself; your ministers take the rest with the usual answer. That is free, but a leader whose call you skipped notices. Some answers cost political capital (a state visit, a hotline).

**Consult your staff** from any crisis card or meeting: the right minister opens with your question already asked. With an AI key in Settings, it is a free-form chat; without one, tap a suggested question (the crisis, your biggest risk, who to meet, the economy, stability, allies) and the minister answers from the game state.

### Hot zones: Gaza and the West Bank

Some places make the news every month without being states. **Gaza** has its own outline on the map and the **West Bank** (the `PSX` state, governed in part by the Palestinian Authority) carries a zone on top of its country. Each zone tracks violence, humanitarian need, aid getting in, civil order and how much ground the Israeli army holds (in the West Bank: settlement and annexation), plus a phase: fragile ceasefire, war, uprising, tense or calm.

- Every month a zone drifts with the situation and usually has one incident in the paper: strikes near the yellow line, aid held at Kerem Shalom, Rafah reopening, clan fighting, the stabilization force arriving, settler attacks, raids in the northern camps, unpaid PA salaries. A Gaza ceasefire can collapse when violence runs high, and mediators can restore one after a war.
- The zones weigh on the countries involved: Israel's stability when violence is high, the West Bank's stability and economy, Egypt during a Gaza war, and opinion against Israel in Europe, the Gulf and beyond when people go hungry.
- **Decisions:** Israel faces ceasefire breaches (targeted strikes, resume the offensive, or the mediators), the crossings, the stabilization force asking for Rafah, Hamas refusing to disarm, settler violence and a sovereignty bill. Egypt decides on Rafah; the United States on famine warnings, the force and the green light for a new war. The West Bank answers raids and an offer to govern Gaza on conditions. Contributors are asked for troops; rich states pledge at the reconstruction conference; everyone else votes at the UN.
- Israel, Egypt and the United States get a standing **Gaza and the West Bank** staff meeting. Click Gaza or a zone's beacon for its card, and ask your Foreign Minister for a line.

Era by era: the 2026 world opens on the fragile October 2025 ceasefire, 2025 opens on the war, 1990 on the first intifada, and 1960 has no zones.

### Political capital ◆

Diplomatic, domestic and covert orders cost **political capital**. You get 2 ◆ a month (3 in a Flashpoint, +1 on Story difficulty) and can bank up to 6. It's meant to be scarce: a reform (2 ◆) is a month's budget, so every month is a choice. Popularity doesn't pay extra capital, and trouble doesn't take it away. You can give at most 6 orders a month (**orders this month**), and each order only once per target. Military hardware and nuclear work cost money as a share of your **defense budget** instead.

### Giving orders

Orders look the same everywhere: the **All orders** list on the desk, the menu that opens when you click a country on the map (its six most relevant orders, then "All orders for ... · same list as the desk"), and a country's dossier. Each order card shows its cost, its chance when the outcome is uncertain, and **whether you can give it before you press it**:

- An order you can't afford says what it needs and what you have ("Needs ◆ 2, you have ◆ 1."), with a fix: a button to remove an order that frees enough, or "wait: +2 next month". The budget it is about lights up (the political capital meter, the orders count, the defense budget).
- An order the situation rules out says why ("Only in wartime. You are not at war with India.", "Ukraine is not a sovereign state in 1990.") and offers a valid order instead when there is one (a ceasefire instead of a war you are already in, Destabilize before an assassination).
- An order already in the month shows **In this month** with **Remove**. **How much?** shows the forecast: effects, risks and costs.

Nothing is refused after the fact for a reason the card could have shown. To change the country, use the picker at the top of the list (type a name, or pick from the suggested countries: the crisis, recent targets, wars, allies, rivals, neighbours) or **Pick on the map**.

Some orders turn the card over to ask again: **Declare war** ("Keep the peace" or "Declare war": there is no undo), a **nuclear strike** (hold the button to authorize), and any order that would fail, endanger or cost a bonus star of your mission ("... anyway?").

| Order | ◆ | Order | ◆ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Court a country | 1 | Reform program | 2 (diminishing returns if repeated within 6 months; raises long-run growth) |
| Denounce | 0 | National address | 1 (less effective if repeated) |
| Offer defense pact | 2 | Spy on / hunt spies | 1 |
| Propose ceasefire | 1 | Sabotage / destabilize / arm rebels | 2 |
| Declare war | 2 | Assassination / back a coup | 3 |

An assassination or coup against a target that is still too protected is refused before it runs, and no capital is spent: an assassination needs the target's stability under 40 or active unrest, a coup needs an armed insurgency or stability under 25. The order card says so and suggests weakening the target first (Destabilize or Arm rebels).

Denouncing is free, but it only rallies voters against an adversary (a historical rival or a country you are at odds with), only the first denunciation each month counts, and it counts less with each month you repeat it within six months. Condemning a partner costs approval, and repeated grandstanding against bystanders starts to cost it too.

## 3. How you lose, and how you see it coming

| Threat | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| **Coup** | A *Coup plot uncovered* crisis. Purge, buy off or concede, before the officers move. |
| **Impeachment / no confidence** | In democracies where the army isn't a political player, there are no coups: an *Impeachment looms* / *A vote of no confidence* card appears instead when legitimacy or stability collapses. Below 10 legitimacy, nothing shields you, not even the promises you made to survive the last plot. |
| **Revolution** | *The streets are boiling* crisis when stability is very low and an insurgency is active. Consolidated democracies only fall to revolution in an armed (guerrilla) uprising with legitimacy under 20. |
| **Defence pacts** | Nobody signs you into a treaty: when a government wants a pact, it lands on your desk as a crisis card. Pacts are rare, never signed with a country at war, and nobody has a formal treaty with Taiwan. If you join an ally's war, you go home when their war ends. |
| **Election defeat** | The state band counts down to the election; *Campaign endgame* card; the screen pulses red if you trail. Voters weigh approval first, then the economy and any war, and a government that has served three years or more pays a "time for a change" penalty. In the 2026 world, foreign elections fall on their real dates (US November 2028, Pakistan February 2029…). |
| **Objective failure** | The Mission button in the state band counts the goals on track every month; the Mission sheet shows each one live (pending, met, failed), and an order or option that would break one says so before you choose it. |
| **Nuclear holocaust** | Using a nuclear weapon can trigger retaliation chains. Don't. |

A red heartbeat around the screen means you are in danger: approval, stability or legitimacy near collapse, or a plot in motion.

## 4. The world fights back

- **Crises with memory.** Some choices have delayed consequences: deny a scandal and a second leak may follow; reject an ultimatum and you'll find out if it was a bluff.
- **Shocks.** Comfortable leaders get tested: market crashes, pandemics, terror attacks, disasters.
- **Hidden hands.** Rivals run covert operations against you. If your intelligence detects them, you'll get a *Foreign interference* card.
- **Fog of war.** Foreign GDP, stability, military strength and nuclear progress are **estimates** with confidence ranges. Spy to sharpen them.
- **Wars** have a single front line (0–100). They end in victory, exhaustion, or a ceasefire the other side must accept. A side that is ahead rarely stops, but once it is clearly winning (65+ on its side of the front) it will often take a peace **on its terms**: you lose 6 approval and 5 legitimacy, far less than a defeat (−15 stability, −20 legitimacy, −15 approval, reparations).
- **Foreign meddling.** Rival services destabilize you and arm your rebels; the monthly report says when. *Hunt foreign spies* (1 ◆) foils about 60% of the operations aimed at you for three months.
- **Wild moves answer back.** Bombing a nuclear power in peacetime (even "only" its nuclear sites) is an act of war: it declares war on you, its defence pacts join, world tension jumps and your approval, legitimacy and stability fall. Using a nuclear weapon brings a total arms embargo, every country's view of you collapses (friends included), approval and legitimacy crash at home, fallout spreads, and a nuclear-armed target or its allies may answer in kind (the end of the game). A war won with a nuclear weapon brings no victory rally.
- **Two-strike rule.** A nuclear program still *developing* is crippled by two consecutive successful strikes. Once *tested*, it is beyond reach.

## 5. Scoring

The **grade** follows the verdict: a won session is never below C, a failed or deposed one never above D, and an early failure is an F; within that band, score versus par decides. "Stay in power" is only failed if you were removed.

You play as the era's **real leader** by default (type a name on the title screen to be someone else).

Economy: growth shocks (oil spikes, crises, financial panics) fade back toward each country's structural trend within about a year, and voters judge growth against what is normal for *their* country. The growth figure the game shows is **real growth**: what actually happened to GDP, including war damage, crisis hits and reparations. A ground war on your own soil is ruinous; a distant air and naval campaign costs far less. Defence budgets rise when rivals mobilise, but only to about one and a half times their peacetime level without a war. Stability, legitimacy and approval are hard to keep at the very top and recover slowly from the very bottom.

Four components, 0–50 each: **Economy**, **Security**, **Approval**, **Prestige** (total 0–200, start 100). They drift each month toward what your situation deserves, so sustained performance matters more than one good month. **Why your score moved** (on the Score button and the result card) names the terms behind each part.

### The reveal

The session ends with a reveal in eight short stages. Space or the right arrow moves on, the left arrow goes back, and **Skip to the report** jumps to the end. It plays once per save (**Replay the reveal** from the report).

1. **The count**: one square per month (green steady, amber strained, red on the edge, black the fall).
2. **The verdict**: a stamp (*Mission complete*, *Voted out*, *Four more years*...), your **grade** and **stars**, a leadership archetype read from what you actually did (*Warmonger*, *Shadow Operator*, *Peacemaker*, *Steady Hand*...), and your **Presidency Score** counting up.
3. **Achievements**: the seals you earned ("What you will be remembered for"), each with its rarity, and two you could reach next time. There are 37, some of them infamy seals for what went badly (they never cost score), plus one signature seal per flashpoint side. Rarity (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary) is measured, not guessed: "earned by N% of simulated leaders".
4. **The moments**: your best and worst month, the turning point, what went well and badly.
5. **How you were seen**: approval over time, elections, relations, the press and history's judgement.
6. **The numbers**: five charts and your counts.
7. **The truth**: "The fog lifts": the real figures your intelligence could not see (a rival's nuclear program, a rival's real strength, the covert operations run against you). In a Flashpoint, a table compares your world with what really happened ("In your world", "In the real world", each row marked Better, Same, Worse or Context), with sources.
8. **Ranking**: it opens on your outcome and achievements ("Mission complete, 9 achievements"), then the ladder of simulated leaders on the same seat (do-nothing staff, random staff, competent staff). Your first run reads "Your first run: a ladder of N runs to climb"; later runs give your place among your own runs. Under the ladder, a quiet line says where your score sits ("better than N% of simulated competent leaders"), next to your record on this seat and your personal best. The share card only carries the percentile when it is 50% or more.

The **Presidency Score** (0 to 1000) adds four parts: **Mission** (up to 400, goals met), **Timing** (up to 100, how many months your goals held), **Leadership** (up to 300, your average monthly score) and **Finish** (up to 200, your final score against par). Being removed from office multiplies it by 0.6; a nuclear holocaust leaves nothing. **Career points** weigh it by difficulty (x0.85 Story, x1 Normal, x1.15 Hard).

**Replay and share.** Every game has a seed code (for example `HC-USA-3PX` for a Flashpoint, `T2026-USA-...` for a Term). **Replay same opening** plays the same world and first crisis again; **Compare runs** puts two runs side by side and shows the month your choices split. **Share card** shares the image card (through your device's share sheet when it takes pictures; otherwise the card is saved and the text summary copied, and the button says which), **Save card** / **Phone card** save the landscape (1200 x 630) or phone (1080 x 1350) image, and **Copy text** copies the text summary (verdict, stars, grade, score, the emoji strip and the seed). On the title screen, **Your record** lists your runs (each with **Replay**, the same opening again, and **Report**) and **Play a seed** starts a game from a code.

**Saved games.** Every month autosaves; Saved games lists your games (signed-in and guest games together, and a cleared browser keeps its guest games). Each game has **Replay same opening** (the same seat, seed, What-if and Start-with choices, from month 1), its seed code to copy, and **Branch from this month**: pick an earlier month (counted like the state band, with its date: "Month 10, Aug 2027: before your choices") and a new save starts at the start of that month, rebuilt from the same seed and every choice you made before it, while the original game stays as it was. **Play a seed** is there too. Games saved before this version can be replayed but not branched.

## 6. Learning as you play

You don't need to know who the Houthis are or where the fortress belt is. The game explains the real world it is set in, as much as you want:

- **How well do you follow world news?** Asked once before your first month, and changeable from the book chip at the top or in Settings, **Explanations**:
  - **Guide me**: names you may not know are underlined (a few per paragraph, never names you have already met), and each crisis is shorter before your first choice: a short opening line, the options without their descriptions, and the rest behind **More**.
  - **Some help** (recommended): clean text, an "i" after each paragraph that names its people and places, and **What is going on here?** on every crisis.
  - **I know this**: clean text with a faint "i". The codex is there if you want it.
- **Explainers** open on a tap or click, never on hover: one line, **More** for the full entry, and an "In your game" line on what that person or place is doing now. On a phone they open as a sheet at the bottom.
- **Situation briefings** (**What is going on here?**): who is who, why it matters, a short timeline of what really happened up to the point where the game takes over, and sources. A briefing opens only when you ask for it, at every level; Settings, **Brief me on each new crisis** (off by default), opens it by itself.
- **The codex** (in the drawers): every person, place, group, term, event and situation the game mentions, searchable, with the ones you have just met marked **New**, plus "How the game works". Entries follow the era: you read about Gorbachev's Soviet Union in 1990, not today's Russia.

Your progress (your level, names you have met and opened) stays in your browser; **Clear codex progress** in Settings resets it.

## 7. Tips

- **Read the arrows twice.** The option that helps approval today often costs you legitimacy or allies tomorrow.
- **Don't spam national addresses.** Each repeat within six months works 40% less, and the press notices.
- **Popularity breeds trouble.** Very popular leaders attract scandals and hubris.
- **Allies matter.** Defense pacts deter, and drag you into their wars.
- **Talk to your cabinet.** Each minister reasons from their department and from your intelligence, so they will disagree.
