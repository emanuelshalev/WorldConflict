How it plays

Every month in office has five steps.

One turn is one month. You read the situation, get briefed on the crisis, plan with your advisors, give your orders, and then see what they did to the world.

  1. 1

    Inspect the world

    Read the map before you act.

    The globe shows all 41 countries as they stand today: who is at war and where the front line is, who is allied with whom, who is close to collapse, and who is building a bomb.

    • Switch layers: relations, regimes, military, economy, stability, intelligence and nuclear.
    • Open any country for its leader, its army, its economy and its view of you.
    • Foreign numbers are intelligence estimates with error bars. Spy to sharpen them.
  2. 2

    Get briefed

    A crisis lands on your desk.

    Each month brings a decision only you can make: an ultimatum, a strike, a coup plot, a tanker on fire. You get the situation, three options, and what each one will cost.

    • Every option shows its effects up front: approval, stability, relations, tension.
    • Options that put your mission at risk are flagged.
    • Ignore it, and it resolves on its own, usually badly.
  3. 3

    Consult your advisors

    Plan with your cabinet.

    Your foreign minister, defence minister, intelligence chief and chief of staff each suggest an order and say why. You have political capital for a few orders a month, so choose.

    • About 30 orders: court or denounce a country, offer a defence pact, mass troops on a border, strike, spy, sabotage, back a coup, address the nation, reform.
    • Before you commit, each order shows its odds, its cost and its risks.
  4. 4

    Execute

    Give the orders.

    Press Execute. Your orders go out, and at the same moment 40 other governments make their own moves, each driven by its history, its red lines and what it believes about you.

    • Wars move, economies grow or shrink, elections and coups happen.
    • Same choices with the same seed give the same world: no dice rolls behind your back.
  5. 5

    See it play out

    Watch your month on the news.

    Every order plays as its own broadcast scene: a press briefing, a treaty signing, troops on the move, an airstrike. Each one is stamped success, failure or backfire, with the numbers it moved and how that line of policy has evolved over your term.

    • 20 kinds of scenes, dressed in the flags of the countries involved.
    • Then the World Chronicle: the month's front page leads with what you did.

Game modes

Three ways to play.

Fifteen minutes, or a whole term.

10 to 15 minutes · 12 turns

Flashpoint

One real crisis, one side, one mission. Pick a seat in one of eight flashpoints (the Strait of Hormuz, Ukraine, Taiwan, the Red Sea, Caracas, Scarborough Shoal, Sudan, Kashmir) and meet your objective within a year.

  • A verdict and up to three stars at the end
  • A debrief of what really happened
  • A new Daily Flashpoint every day, the same for everyone
45 to 60 minutes · 48 turns

One Term

Govern any of 41 countries for four years. Survive scandals, shocks, rivals and your own cabinet, then face the voters, or the Party.

  • Win by finishing your term
  • Bonus stars for a strong record and a statecraft goal
  • Saves automatically: play in parts
Several sessions · 120 turns

Legacy

Ten years in power with the full sandbox: wars, coups, nuclear brinkmanship, elections, and the long judgement of history.

  • Every system switched on
  • A history-book report at the end
  • Built for long campaigns

World Conflict

World Conflict is a work of fiction and a political simulation. It draws on public reporting up to October 2026 and portrays real public figures in an alternate history; their in-game words and decisions are invented and imply no endorsement. Game scenes are illustrations, not footage. Not affiliated with any government, leader or news organisation.
