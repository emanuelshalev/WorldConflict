One turn = one month

One month. One crisis. Every choice costs something.

01

The crisis

A card lands on your desk with two or three options. Each one shows the direction of its consequences (Approval ▲▲, Tension ▼) but never the exact numbers. Each option is backed by a minister, and your ministers disagree. If you ignore the card, it resolves on its own, usually badly.

02

Your orders

Court an ally, denounce a rival, hunt foreign spies, propose a ceasefire. Read the map through seven intelligence layers first. Diplomatic, domestic and covert orders cost political capital, and you get 2◆ a month. A reform takes all of it, so every month means choosing what to leave undone.

03

The consequences

Press Execute. The World Chronicle leads with your decision, then shows what moved and why. Some choices come back months later: a denied scandal leaks again, or a rejected ultimatum turns out not to have been a bluff.

Situation room · after hours

The world of November 2026. Your desk.

A Supreme Leader killed in an air strike. A deposed president facing charges in New York. A local ceasefire in occupied Ukraine. Coast guards with water cannon at a reef 220 km from Manila. You read these headlines this year. Now you have to answer them.

World Conflict is a turn-based simulation where every country is an agent with its own leader, interests and memory. One turn is one month. There are no dead turns and no clean options. You get consequences, and most of them arrive late, in public.

Real events · alternate history

It really happened. Then you took office.

Every flashpoint opens on events that really happened. Where it goes from there is an alternate history, and you write it.

    Dates and facts come from public reporting. Opening scenes inside the game, such as a sailor's death at Scarborough or an attack near Jammu, are fiction written to start the scenario, and the game labels them as such. The real incidents at Scarborough stopped short of lethal force. After every Flashpoint, a debrief tells you what really happened. Real events up to October 2026; everything after is fiction.

    Built on the real world

    Real leaders. Imperfect intelligence. Coups you can see coming.

    • Real leaders, real calendars.

      You play the leader actually in office, or type your own name. Foreign elections fall on their real dates, and term limits apply.

    • Fog of war, for everyone.

      Foreign GDP, stability, military strength and nuclear progress are estimates with confidence ranges. Your rivals see you the same way. Spy to sharpen the picture. Miscalculation is part of the design.

    • Danger is telegraphed.

      Before a coup there is a plot. Before a revolution the streets boil. Impeachments and no-confidence votes arrive as crisis cards before they strike. When you fall, you will have seen it coming.

    • Plausible, not prophetic.

      Every government acts on its history, its interests and its red lines. What happens next is an alternate history you could believe, not a prediction.

    • Hard options, real costs.

      Covert action, assassination and nuclear weapons exist in the game because they exist in the world. They are expensive, rarely work, and the world remembers: a nuclear strike brings a global embargo and can end the session for everyone.

    • AI you control.

      The built-in algorithmic leaders are free, run offline and are reproducible. You can also let an LLM (Claude, OpenAI, Gemini or a local model) take over key decisions, with the cost shown up front and a spending cap you set.

    ⚠ Existential · eyes only

    You will see it coming.

    A red heartbeat around the screen means you are in danger: approval, stability or legitimacy near collapse, or a plot in motion. Purge, buy off or concede, before the officers move.
